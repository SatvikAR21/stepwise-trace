# Annual Supplier Risk Review 2025

**Organization:** Kestrel Foods Inc.
**Prepared by:** Procurement Risk Office
**Review period:** January 1, 2025 to June 30, 2025
**Lead reviewer:** Marta Kowalczyk, Head of Supplier Quality

## 1. Introduction
This review covers the ten suppliers that together provide 82% of Kestrel Foods' raw ingredients by spend. Each supplier was assessed on delivery performance, quality incidents, financial stability, and compliance with our Supplier Code of Conduct. Ratings use a four-level scale: Low, Moderate, Elevated, and Critical. Supplier-level detail appears in Section 2. Overall conclusions and required actions appear in Section 3, which must be read by all decision makers before any contract renewal is approved.

### 1.1 Methodology
Delivery performance was measured from receiving-dock timestamps against the delivery windows agreed in each purchase order; a delivery counts as on time if it arrives no more than two hours after the window closes. Quality incidents were taken from the receiving inspection log and classified as minor (no product risk, administrative or cosmetic), moderate (potential product risk, contained before use) or major (product risk not contained). Financial stability was assessed from public filings where available and otherwise from credit-agency reports and supplier-provided statements. Code of Conduct compliance was assessed through scheduled on-site audits by our own quality team, supplemented by third-party certification records. Where a supplier had no audit in the period, the most recent audit result is used and flagged.

### 1.2 Market conditions
The first half of 2025 was shaped by three external factors. First, grain and oilseed prices remained volatile after a poor harvest in two major exporting regions, which pushed several suppliers to request price reviews. Second, ocean freight rates stabilized but port congestion on the Gulf Coast continued to delay imported ingredients by an average of six days. Third, new state-level food-traceability rules came into force in April, requiring lot-level records for all high-risk ingredients. Most suppliers adapted to these rules without difficulty, and our receiving team now rejects any high-risk lot that arrives without traceability data. These conditions are reflected in the delivery and financial scores below and should be taken into account when comparing results with the 2024 review.

### 1.3 Scope exclusions
Packaging-only vendors below $500,000 in annual spend, logistics providers, and equipment maintenance contractors are outside the scope of this review and are covered by the separate Indirect Procurement Review. Suppliers onboarded after January 1, 2025 are also excluded because they do not yet have six months of performance history; they will be included in the 2026 review.

## 2. Supplier Assessments

### 2.1 Prairie Mills Flour Co.
Annual spend: $4.2 million. On-time delivery: 98.1%. Quality incidents: 1 minor (mislabeled pallet). Financial outlook: stable, with a new credit facility secured in March. Code of Conduct audit: passed. Risk rating: Low. Prairie Mills remains our most reliable flour supplier and has offered a 2% volume discount for the next contract year.

### 2.2 Sunridge Dairy Cooperative
Annual spend: $3.6 million. On-time delivery: 96.4%. Quality incidents: 2 minor (temperature logging gaps during transport). Financial outlook: stable. Code of Conduct audit: passed. Risk rating: Low. Sunridge has installed new refrigerated-truck telemetry, which should close the logging gaps by the end of the year.

### 2.3 Coastal Sugar Refiners
Annual spend: $2.9 million. On-time delivery: 91.7%. Quality incidents: none. Financial outlook: moderate concern after a 12% revenue decline caused by lower export demand. Code of Conduct audit: passed. Risk rating: Moderate. We recommend monitoring their quarterly filings and keeping a qualified secondary sugar source available.

### 2.4 Verdant Valley Produce
Annual spend: $2.4 million. On-time delivery: 94.2%. Quality incidents: 3 minor (bruised stock rejected at receiving). Financial outlook: stable. Code of Conduct audit: passed with one observation on seasonal worker housing, now resolved. Risk rating: Low. Their new packing facility opened in May and has improved consistency.

### 2.5 Blue Ridge Poultry Farms
Annual spend: $2.1 million. On-time delivery: 97.3%. Quality incidents: 1 minor (late certificate of analysis). Financial outlook: stable. Code of Conduct audit: passed. Risk rating: Low. Blue Ridge has agreed to provide certificates electronically before each shipment.

### 2.6 Cortez Spice Imports
Annual spend: $1.8 million. On-time delivery: 88.9%. Quality incidents: 1 moderate (foreign material found in a cumin lot, contained before use). Financial outlook: stable. Code of Conduct audit: passed. Risk rating: Moderate. Port congestion caused most delays; Cortez is adding a second shipping route through Houston.

### 2.7 Northern Oils Ltd.
Annual spend: $1.5 million. On-time delivery: 99.0%. Quality incidents: none. Financial outlook: strong. Code of Conduct audit: passed. Risk rating: Low. Northern Oils is our best-performing supplier this period and a candidate for a longer-term agreement.

### 2.8 Greenway Packaging Solutions
Annual spend: $1.3 million. On-time delivery: 95.5%. Quality incidents: 2 minor (misprinted lot codes). Financial outlook: stable. Code of Conduct audit: passed. Risk rating: Low. Greenway is introducing recyclable film that supports our 2026 sustainability targets.

### 2.9 Summit Egg Producers
Annual spend: $1.1 million. On-time delivery: 93.8%. Quality incidents: none. Financial outlook: moderate concern due to feed-cost inflation. Code of Conduct audit: passed. Risk rating: Moderate. Summit has requested a 6% price increase, currently under negotiation.

### 2.10 Halvorsen Grain Cooperative
Annual spend: $2.3 million. On-time delivery: 97.9%. Delivery and financial metrics are in line with expectations. An unannounced food-safety audit was completed in June; its results are covered in Section 3.

## 3. Conclusions and Required Actions

### 3.1 Critical finding: Halvorsen Grain Cooperative
The June unannounced audit found Salmonella contamination in two storage silos, falsified cleaning records covering at least four months, and no functioning pest-control program. Two lots already delivered to our Dayton plant tested positive and were destroyed. Risk rating: Critical. Our total exposure, including destroyed inventory and production downtime, is estimated at $2.3 million.

### 3.2 Required actions
1. Suspend all purchases from Halvorsen Grain Cooperative immediately and do not renew its contract.
2. Recall and test all Halvorsen-sourced oat products produced since March 1, 2025.
3. Qualify Prairie Mills Flour Co. as the interim grain supplier within 30 days.
4. Notify the Food Safety Committee and the insurance carrier by July 15, 2025.

### 3.3 Overall assessment
Apart from Halvorsen, the supplier base is healthy. However, the Halvorsen finding is a critical food-safety risk and must be resolved before any other renewal decisions are made.
